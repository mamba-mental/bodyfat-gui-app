# Two-Week Cut: Consolidated, Evidence-Checked Plan

**Purpose:** Maximize visible fat loss and muscular definition over 14 days while preserving strength and lean mass.

**Working assumptions for the example numbers:** 5'9", approximately 245 lb, experienced resistance trainee. Your recent stated range has been roughly 240-250 lb, so 245 lb is used only as the calculation example. If your current seven-day average is materially different, use the formulas in the next section.

## Bottom line

The three source packages agree on the fundamentals:

1. Create a meaningful but controlled calorie deficit.
2. Keep protein high.
3. Continue hard resistance training to preserve muscle.
4. Add short, repeatable cardio sessions and maintain daily movement.
5. Expect the scale to fall quickly at first from glycogen, water, and gut-content changes, then more slowly.

The best combined protocol is **not** the most extreme version of all three plans stacked together. Eight high-volume lifting sessions, daily hard intervals, ultra-low carbohydrate intake, stimulant fat burners, and fluid restriction would create more fatigue and risk than additional fat loss.

### What to expect

- A sensible target for a trained 245 lb person is approximately **0.5-1.0% of bodyweight per week**, or roughly **2.5-5 lb over 14 days**. Some of that will be water and glycogen, especially in the first four days.
- Joe Delaney's six-pound scale change in the supplied video is plausible, but it should not be interpreted as six pounds of body fat. His own account describes a large early drop and a slower decline afterward.
- Two weeks can make an already-trained physique visibly tighter. It cannot safely create a stage-ready physique from a high starting body-fat level.

## 1. Nutrition targets

### Step 1: Set calories

Use your verified maintenance intake when possible:

> **Daily cut calories = maintenance calories minus 20-25%**

An equivalent starting deficit is approximately **500-750 kcal/day**. For the working 245 lb example, use **2,450 kcal/day only if maintenance is approximately 3,000-3,200 kcal/day**.

Do not automatically use the T Nation prescription of less than 10 kcal per pound. Its example also uses 1.5 g protein per pound of total bodyweight, which would force an unnecessary 368 g protein/day at 245 lb.

### Step 2: Set macros

For a trained person in a short calorie deficit:

- **Protein:** 0.85-1.0 g per pound of bodyweight when relatively lean. If body fat is above roughly 25%, use 0.9-1.0 g per pound of goal weight instead. A useful working target here is **220 g/day**.
- **Fat:** Keep a practical floor of approximately 0.25-0.35 g per pound of goal weight. Working target: **70 g/day**.
- **Carbohydrate:** Use the remaining calories. Carbs support training performance and do not need to be driven to near zero.

#### Working targets at 245 lb

| Day type | Calories | Protein | Carbohydrate | Fat | Purpose |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Standard cut day | 2,450 | 220 g | 235 g | 70 g | Main fat-loss intake |
| Controlled high-carb day | 2,820 | 220 g | 350 g | 60 g | Performance, glycogen, adherence |
| Appearance day | 2,700-2,900 | 200-220 g | 320-380 g | 45-60 g | Optional fullness, not extra fat loss |

The controlled high-carb day is a training-support tool, not a metabolic loophole. It should remain at or slightly below maintenance and should not become an untracked cheat day.

### If bodyweight differs from 245 lb

1. Set calories at 75-80% of verified maintenance.
2. Set protein using the rule above.
3. Set fat using goal weight.
4. Calculate carbohydrate:

> **Carbohydrate grams = (daily calories - protein grams x 4 - fat grams x 9) / 4**

### Meal structure

Use **four meals** rather than forcing six. Four meals make adherence and tracking easier while distributing protein adequately.

| Meal | Protein | Carbs | Fat | Example foods |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Breakfast | 50-55 g | 50-60 g | 15-20 g | Eggs/egg whites, oats, berries |
| Lunch | 50-60 g | 60-70 g | 15-20 g | Chicken or turkey, rice, large salad, olive oil |
| Pre/post-workout meal | 50-55 g | 45-60 g | 10-15 g | Greek yogurt and whey, fruit, cereal or bagel |
| Dinner | 55-65 g | 55-70 g | 20-25 g | Lean fish/beef, potatoes or rice, vegetables |
| **Daily total** | **220 g** | **235 g** | **70 g** | **2,450 kcal** |

Use food labels and weighed portions to hit the totals; the exact macros of meat, dairy, cereal, and cooking oils vary by brand and preparation.

### Food rules for all 14 days

- Weigh calorie-dense foods: oils, nuts, nut butter, cheese, dressings, cereal, rice, and snacks.
- Build each meal around lean protein.
- Use potatoes, rice, oats, fruit, and vegetables for most carbohydrates.
- Put roughly 25-35% of daily carbs in the meal before training and 25-35% after training.
- Keep vegetables high on standard days for fullness and micronutrients.
- Keep alcohol at zero for the 14 days.
- Keep restaurant meals to zero or use a meal whose calories and portions can be verified.
- Sugar-free beverages are acceptable if they do not trigger appetite or gastrointestinal symptoms.

### High-carb day changes

On Days 6 and, only if there is a Day 14 appearance target, Day 13:

- Keep protein unchanged.
- Reduce added fats by about 10 g.
- Add approximately 115 g carbohydrate from familiar, low-fat foods such as rice, potatoes, oats, bagels, cereal, and fruit.
- Do not use pizza, burgers, or an unrestricted junk meal. The T Nation suggestion can produce unpredictable bloating and overshoot calories.

### Cardio intensity

Use a conversational pace, approximately RPE 4-6 out of 10. Because prescription stimulants and caffeine can alter heart rate, the talk test is more useful than chasing a heart-rate-zone number. The goal is repeatable calorie expenditure without reducing lifting performance.

If current steps are below 7,000/day, add roughly 2,000 steps to the current baseline instead of jumping immediately to 12,000.

### Week 2 progression rule

- Keep **1-2 reps in reserve** on every compound set.
- If every Week 1 rep was completed at the prescribed tempo and recovery is good, increase the load by **2.5-5%** in Week 2.
- Alternatively, add one set to the first two movements only. Do **not** increase load and volume simultaneously.
- If strength drops by more than 5%, sleep is poor, or soreness persists longer than 48 hours, keep the original load and remove one accessory set.
- Do not introduce unfamiliar exercises, forced reps, drop sets, or high-eccentric novelty during this 14-day block.

### Optional Day 14 pump circuit

Perform two rounds, stopping 3-4 reps before failure:

1. Push-ups: 15-20
2. Band or cable rows: 15-20
3. Lateral raises: 15-20
4. Curls: 12-15
5. Pressdowns: 12-15

Rest 30-45 seconds between movements. Finish 20-40 minutes before photos or the event. This is for temporary muscular fullness, not fat loss.

## 4. Hydration, sodium, and the final 24 hours

### Keep water and sodium consistent

Do **not** follow the supplied T Nation instructions to stop drinking at 8 p.m., sip as little as possible on the event day, remove sodium, then abruptly reintroduce it. Water and electrolyte manipulation can impair performance, cause cramping or dizziness, and produce a flatter or less predictable look.

- Drink normally and consistently throughout the 14 days.
- Replace fluid lost during training and use urine color, thirst, and bodyweight change as practical feedback.
- Keep sodium intake consistent from day to day; do not abruptly cut or load salt.
- Do not use diuretics, laxatives, sauna dehydration, alcohol, or potassium loading.

### Optional appearance-day protocol

This is appropriate only if Day 14 is a photo, beach, or presentation target. If not, use the standard cut day.

**Day 13**

- Use the controlled high-carb macros.
- Choose only foods already tolerated.
- After dinner, reduce unusually bulky, high-fiber, or high-FODMAP foods if they predictably cause abdominal distention. Do not eliminate all produce or fiber earlier in the plan.
- Keep normal water and sodium.

**Day 14**

- Eat three or four familiar, lower-fat meals.
- Two to three hours before the target time, have approximately 50-75 g carbohydrate, 30-40 g lean protein, and a normal amount of salt.
- Continue normal drinking based on thirst; do not force or restrict fluids.
- Avoid carbonated drinks if they make you look or feel bloated.
- Use the optional pump circuit; do not perform exhausting cardio or leg training.

## 5. Supplements

### Keep

- **Creatine monohydrate:** 3-5 g daily if already using it. No loading phase is needed. Continue it through Day 14; intracellular water can support muscular fullness. If you have kidney disease or have been told to limit creatine/protein, obtain medical guidance first.
- **Protein powder:** Use only as a convenient way to meet the protein target.
- **Psyllium:** Optional on standard days if needed for fiber and tolerated. Take it with adequate water and separate it from medications according to the product or pharmacist's instructions. Skip it in the final evening if it causes distention.

### Do not add

- Hot-Rox or another thermogenic fat burner
- High-stimulant pre-workout
- Ephedrine-like products, DMAA/DMHA products, or yohimbine stacks
- Diuretics, laxatives, or "water-loss" supplements
- A new supplement during the final week

Given reported Adderall and coffee use, do not stack additional stimulants. Maintain only the caffeine amount already tolerated and approved by the prescriber; do not increase it to compensate for low calories or poor sleep.

## 6. Recovery rules

- Target **7.5-9 hours in bed** with a fixed wake time.
- If sleep is under six hours, remove intervals entirely and reduce accessory volume by approximately 25% that day.
- Keep hard sessions to 60-75 minutes.
- Use light walking and mobility on recovery days; no hidden boot-camp sessions.
- Schedule meals, training, and bedtime in advance. The plan works through consistency, not last-minute compensation.

## 7. Measurement and adjustment system

### Daily

- Weigh after waking, using the bathroom, and before food or fluids.
- Log calories, protein, steps, training, sleep, and morning resting heart rate.
- If available, check blood pressure at the same time of day, especially because stimulant medication, caffeine, calorie restriction, and cardio can all affect cardiovascular response.

### Days 1, 7, and 14

- Measure waist at the navel, relaxed, after exhaling normally.
- Take front, side, and back photos with identical lighting, distance, time of day, and pose.
- Do not rely on a consumer body-fat scale as an absolute measurement. Use it only as a noisy trend alongside waist, photos, and performance.

### Adjustment checkpoint: morning of Day 6

- If the three-day average is not falling and tracking is verified, first audit oils, snacks, restaurant estimates, and portion weights.
- If intake is accurate, reduce calories by **150-200/day** or add **10 minutes** to three cardio sessions. Do not do both at once.
- If bodyweight is falling faster than 1.25% per week after the initial four-day water shift **and** performance or recovery is deteriorating, add **150-250 kcal/day**, primarily from carbohydrate.

### Stop the cut and seek medical care when appropriate

Stop training and obtain prompt medical assessment for chest pain, fainting, severe shortness of breath, new or sustained palpitations, confusion, severe headache, repeated vomiting, inability to keep fluids down, or severe weakness/cramping that does not resolve. Do not use this plan without clinician guidance if there is uncontrolled hypertension, significant heart or kidney disease, diabetes medication with hypoglycemia risk, an active eating disorder, or a medically prescribed fluid/electrolyte restriction.

| --- | --- | --- |
| Joe Delaney video/transcript | Approximately 600 kcal food deficit, about 1 g protein/lb, moderate fat, carbs filling the remainder, 20-30 min daily treadmill work, realistic discussion of water-weight changes | Six lifting days reduced to four structured sessions per week; his individual 2,400 kcal target is not universal |

| T Nation "2 Weeks to Shredded" | Resistance training during a deficit, planned high-carb training days, 5 g/day creatine, emphasis on nutrition | Excessive protein, repeated very-low-carb days, daily moderate-to-high-intensity cardio, stimulant fat burner, sodium removal, water restriction, and junk-food peak meal rejected |

## 9. Non-negotiable checklist

- [ ] Calories and protein logged every day
- [ ] Food weighed, including oils and condiments
- [ ] Four lifting sessions per week completed with 1-2 reps in reserve
- [ ] Cardio kept conversational and repeatable
- [ ] Steps kept consistent
- [ ] No alcohol
- [ ] No stimulant fat burner or new pre-workout
- [ ] Water and sodium kept consistent
- [ ] Sleep protected; volume reduced after severe sleep loss
- [ ] Day 1, 7, and 14 measurements taken identically
